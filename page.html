Recurring segment

Uro404: myth not found

Every week we look up a popular urology "fact" and check whether it actually exists.

"Cranberry juice cures a UTI."

It won't treat an infection that has already started. Evidence on cranberry for preventing repeat UTIs is mixed. An active UTI usually needs a doctor and often antibiotics.

404 · cure not found
"Beer helps you pass a kidney stone."

Alcohol can dehydrate you, which works against you. Water is the drink that matters for stones.

404 · evidence not found
"A vasectomy lowers your testosterone."

A vasectomy blocks the tubes that carry sperm. Hormone production in the testicles carries on as before.

Kidney stones 101: why they happen and how they pass

If you've had one, you remember it. Here's what's actually going on inside.

What is a kidney stone?

Your pee carries dissolved minerals. When there's too much mineral and not enough water, those minerals can clump into tiny crystals. Over weeks or months, the crystals can grow into a hard little pebble. That's a stone.

Why does it hurt so much?

Most stones sit quietly in the kidney. The pain usually starts when a stone moves into the ureter, the narrow tube that carries pee to your bladder, and blocks the flow. The kidney swells behind the blockage, which causes the classic waves of pain in the side or back.

Size matters

Doctors measure stones in millimetres. As a rough guide:

SizeAbout as big asUsually
Under 5 mmA grain of riceOften passes on its own with time and fluids
5–10 mmA peaMay pass, sometimes with medicine to help
Over 10 mmA chickpea or biggerOften needs a procedure to break up or remove

The one habit that helps most

Drink enough water that your pee stays pale most of the day. More water means more dilute pee, which makes it harder for crystals to form.

When to see a doctor straight away
  • Pain so bad you can't sit still or get comfortable
  • Fever or chills along with the pain
  • Nausea or vomiting that stops you keeping fluids down
  • Blood in your pee, or you can't pee at all
